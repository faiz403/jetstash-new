# Weekly fare sweep — 3 November 2026 (fare recovery)

**Why this sweep exists.** On 3 November 2026, routes with a current Fare Signal fell from 62 of 89 to 1 of 89. Every published
fare except Birmingham–Islamabad's departed on 17 November 2026, and the 15-day departure guard (`lib/freshness-thresholds.ts`)
correctly withdrew them all on the same day. The CEO approved this sweep the same morning: fresh, verifiable evidence only,
the 15-day rule unchanged, nothing old made to look new.

- **Collected:** 3 November 2026, 01:11–02:13 UTC (01:11–02:13 GMT), in the founder's Chrome through Claude in Chrome, Google Flights.
- **Search:** URLs generated by `node scripts/fare-sweep/generate-sweep-urls.mjs 2026-11-03` (FARE-METHOD-001: departure = sweep date +
  56 days). Outbound Tuesday 29 December 2026, return Tuesday 12 January 2027, 1 adult, Economy, GBP, exact airports, Cheapest tab.
- **Peak season:** the generator flags this window as `christmas-new-year`. These fares are not directly comparable with fares for
  another travel window, so every record carries its own profile, `{route}-economy-1adult-christmas-new-year-2026-v1`, instead of the
  route's routine series. Fare Watcher only compares records with the same profile, so it builds no baseline from autumn fares.
  (With the routine profile, Bristol–Marrakech's £80 would have been flagged internally as 30% below a £114 median of
  October/November travel fares.) Public copy shows each fare's travel dates and is otherwise unchanged.
- **Scope:** 42 of 89 routes. 15 commercial-priority routes first, then the 27 other routes whose route status stays publishable
  into December. The remaining 47 were not searched, because their route review has already lapsed or lapses within days, so a fare
  would be hidden almost at once. They need route re-verification first (see "Route verification is the next limit" below).
- **Loads:** 47 Google Flights search pages (one per route, plus a second load for LHR–JED, BRS–FAO, BRS–FCO and BRS–RAK). Selecting
  flights and reading booking options happened inside each page. No booking flow was entered, nothing was purchased, no affiliate link was clicked.
- **Screenshots:** none were saved. Evidence is the text read from each results page, return list and booking page, recorded
  below at the time of the check.

## Selection rules (applied to every route)

1. **Exact airports only.** A row counts only when it is the route's own airport pair on both legs.
2. **Cheapest useful clean round trip.** Take the cheapest outbound without a "Self transfer" or "Separate tickets" badge, then the
   cheapest such return for it. Useful means: long-haul total journey under 24 hours with every layover under 8 hours; short-haul
   (Europe, Turkey, Morocco) under 10 hours with every layover under 5 hours. If that outbound has no useful return, try the next
   one (up to three). Cheaper rows that fail the rule are recorded below, never published.
3. **Clean is decided per booking offer** (7 October 2026 rule). Where the cheapest nonstop rows carried a row badge, the nonstop
   pair was opened and the cheapest offer without a "Separate tickets booked together" label was taken.
4. **A single-ticket itinerary must be sold by the airline too.** If no airline offer exists for a mixed-airline itinerary (only
   online agents sell it), the route is held.
5. **Outlier guard.** If the cheapest unlabelled offer is more than 10% below the next unlabelled offer, it is not published;
   the route is held.
6. **Recorded on every published record:** both legs' times, journey length, stops and connection airport; Google's baggage line
   for the itinerary (or `not stated`); the airline's own price for the same flights. The archive record's `sourceUrl` is the
   generated search URL. The selected flights and the agent that sold the lowest offer are recorded here only, never in the
   traveller-facing note.

## Why the 13 and 20 October sweeps never reached the site

- **13 October:** all 89 routes were read, but the qualification found that an unbadged cheapest row does not prove a clean round trip.
  The founder accepted it as observational evidence only. A small verified batch (BRS–RAK, LGW–FCO) was proposed and never approved.
- **20 October:** all 89 routes were read with search-row rules ("search prices only, not bookable and not verified"). The B2 pilot
  verified MAN–ISB and MAN–DXB at booking level, but the founder chose not to publish the MAN–ISB £1,080 or schedule a 3 November
  re-check, and work moved to the SEO audit, the homepage and commercial week.
- **Underneath:** collection and publication were separate steps, and publication waited for a decision with no deadline. Nothing
  watched when the live fares would expire. The live set came from the 6 October sweep, whose saved searches reused fixed 17 November
  dates; the rolling-window fix (19 October) only applied to sweeps that were never published. The 15-day guard (17 October) moved
  that cliff from 17 November to 3 November, and nobody recalculated it.
